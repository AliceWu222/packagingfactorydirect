Packaging Tooling & Die Cost Guide

What one-time tooling costs should buyers expect for custom packaging?

Direct answer

Most custom packaging needs one-time tooling: steel rule dies for cartons, rotary dies for bags and labels, gravure cylinders for film printing, and assembly fixtures for rigid boxes. Tooling is a fixed cost charged once and reused on reorders, and it is a main reason MOQ exists.

RFQ checklist

  • Which tooling applies to your structure
  • Tooling cost quoted separately or amortized
  • Tooling ownership and storage terms
  • Reuse on reorders without new charge
  • Tooling lead time

FAQ

Do I own the dies after paying for them?

Typically the dies are made for your order and stored by the factory for your reuse. Confirm ownership and storage terms in the RFQ.

Can tooling cost be avoided?

Digital printing and standard existing dies avoid tooling for some products. Custom sizes and structures usually need a die.

Are tooling costs refunded or amortized?

Factories usually amortize tooling into the unit price or charge it separately. Ask for both options and compare.

How long does die making take?

Steel rule dies typically take a few days; gravure cylinders take longer. Include tooling time in your lead-time plan.
