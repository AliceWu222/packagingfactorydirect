Packaging Bleed, Trim & Safe Zone Guide

Three measurements that decide whether artwork prints correctly.

Direct answer

Bleed is extra artwork extending past the cut line (usually 2-3mm), trim is where the box is actually cut, and the safe zone is where critical text and logos must stay. Setting all three correctly prevents white edges and cut-off text.

RFQ checklist

  • Extend background graphics to bleed
  • Keep text and logos in the safe zone
  • Check folds do not interrupt critical text
  • Confirm bleed settings with the factory
  • Review a digital or physical proof

FAQ

What happens if there is no bleed?

Cutting tolerance can expose white edges, which looks unfinished and unprofessional.

How much bleed do I need?

2-3mm is standard for packaging; confirm with the factory for your structure and press.

What is the safe zone?

The inner margin where text and logos must stay so cutting and folding never clip them.

Can you fix my artwork bleed?

Basic bleed extensions can be prepared for you; complex designs are best fixed in the source file before export.
