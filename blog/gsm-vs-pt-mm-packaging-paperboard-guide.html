GSM vs PT vs mm: Packaging Paperboard Guide

Separate mass from thickness before comparing folding carton, rigid box and corrugated packaging quotations.

Direct answer: GSM and thickness are not the same property. GSM means grams per square meter and describes paper or board mass per unit area. PT (points) and mm (millimeters) describe caliper or thickness. One point equals 0.001 inch, or exactly 0.0254 mm, but there is no universal conversion from GSM to points or millimeters. Board composition, coating, compression, moisture and bulk can produce different calipers at a similar grammage. For a comparable custom-box RFQ, specify the grade, grammage where relevant, measured caliper and tolerance, construction, converting processes and approval evidence.

Content owner: Packaging Factory Direct. Published and reviewed: August 14, 2026. This is a purchasing and specification guide, not a declaration that a particular board is suitable for a product, regulation or distribution system.

Key takeaways for packaging buyers

  • Use GSM to describe mass per area and PT or mm to describe thickness.
  • Convert points to millimeters mathematically, but do not convert GSM to thickness without board-specific data.
  • Do not compare folding carton, rigid-box greyboard and corrugated board using one number alone.
  • Identify the grade, manufacturer or approved-equivalent rules, test method, tolerance and converting process.
  • Approve the actual printed, creased, folded and assembled sample for the intended product.

ISO 536 specifies a method for determining paper and board grammage. ISO 534 addresses thickness, density and specific volume. TAPPI/ANSI T 410 and T 411 similarly treat grammage and caliper as separate measurements. That separation is the reason a supplier should not silently replace one property with the other.

Exact point-to-millimeter conversion

TAPPI's writing guidance defines one point as 0.001 inch. Because one inch is exactly 25.4 millimeters, one point is exactly 0.0254 mm. The following values are mathematical unit conversions, not claims about the measured tolerance or performance of a real sheet.

Why GSM cannot be converted to thickness universally

Grammage tells a buyer how much a defined area weighs. Caliper tells a buyer how thick the sheet is under a defined measurement method. The relationship between them depends on apparent density or specific volume. A denser sheet can place similar mass into less thickness; a bulkier sheet can occupy more thickness at a similar mass.

Fiber mix, number of plies, fillers, coatings, calendaring or compression, moisture and production variation can affect this relationship. A generic chart that maps every 350 GSM board to one point value therefore removes information that the buyer needs. Use a current technical data sheet or measured sample for the exact grade, and state the method and tolerance when thickness is critical.

Do not specify every packaging structure the same way

Folding cartons

A folding-carton RFQ should identify the paperboard grade or an approval process for equivalents, grammage where relevant, target caliper and tolerance, coated side, grain direction, surface requirements and converting operations. Foil, embossing, lamination, heavy ink coverage, scoring and folding can change how the converted carton behaves. The product load, panel geometry and closure matter alongside the board.

Wrapped rigid boxes

A wrapped rigid box normally combines a structural board with a separate wrap paper, adhesive and sometimes an insert. Specify the structural greyboard thickness in mm or points, its tolerance and construction; then specify the wrap paper and decoration separately. Adding the wrap-paper GSM to the greyboard thickness is not a meaningful shortcut. Verify corners, hinge movement, lid fit, wrapped edges and insert height on an assembled sample.

Corrugated mailers and shipping boxes

Corrugated board is a system of liners, fluted medium and adhesive. State the intended board construction, flute or combination, relevant component specifications, box style, dimensions, joint and the required application-specific evidence. A total GSM figure alone cannot communicate flute geometry, liner arrangement, converting quality or finished-box performance. Review the packed product and distribution route rather than selecting corrugated material by a single paper number.

Sample approval and incoming quality checks

  1. Confirm the data source: request the current technical data for the exact proposed grade, not a generic family name.
  2. Agree on methods and tolerances: record whether grammage, caliper or both are acceptance properties and how they will be measured.
  3. Review the blank: check grain, surface, print side and planned crease or fold direction.
  4. Review the decorated sheet: confirm that coatings, lamination, foil and embossing are represented.
  5. Approve the converted box: evaluate dimensions, fit, panels, closures, appearance and packing process with the intended product.
  6. Retain a controlled reference: identify the revision and store records or samples under conditions appropriate to the project.
  7. Inspect production consistently: use the agreed sample plan, tools, conditioning and decision rules; do not compare one uncontrolled reading with a nominal marketing number.

Common GSM and thickness specification errors

  • Using GSM as a synonym for thickness: the two properties answer different questions.
  • Using an online GSM-to-PT chart as a material standard: it cannot know the actual board's density, coating or manufacturing data.
  • Calling a point value a grammage: points express thickness, not grams per square meter.
  • Specifying a rigid box by wrap-paper GSM only: the structural board, adhesive and assembled construction remain undefined.
  • Specifying corrugated board by total GSM only: flute, liner arrangement, box design and application evidence remain undefined.
  • Assuming higher GSM always means stronger packaging: material composition, geometry, conversion and use conditions also affect performance.
  • Approving an unprinted blank as the final reference: decoration and conversion can change the finished box.

FAQ about GSM, points and millimeters

Is GSM the same as paperboard thickness?

No. GSM is mass per unit area, while points and millimeters describe thickness or caliper. Two boards with similar grammage can have different thickness because their furnish, coating, compression, moisture and bulk can differ.

Can GSM be converted directly to points or millimeters?

Not from GSM alone. A valid relationship requires the specific board's measured caliper or apparent density under defined test conditions. A generic online conversion can misstate the actual material.

How many millimeters are in one paper point?

One point is 0.001 inch, which is exactly 0.0254 millimeter as a unit conversion. This converts a stated thickness unit; it does not predict the thickness of a board from its GSM.

What should a folding-carton paperboard RFQ specify?

Identify the board grade or approved equivalent, grammage where relevant, target caliper and tolerance, coated side, grain direction, print and finish processes, converting requirements, product load and the approved sample or measurable acceptance criteria.

Does higher GSM always make a stronger box?

No. Grammage alone does not determine box performance. Fiber composition, board structure, caliper, moisture, grain direction, creasing, geometry, adhesive, print and finish processes, product load and distribution conditions also matter. Validate the finished pack for its intended use.
