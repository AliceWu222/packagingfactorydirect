Editorial and Source Policy

This page explains who owns published buyer guidance, how sources are selected, how evidence is separated from marketing claims, and how readers can request a correction.

Policy in one paragraph

Packaging Factory Direct is the organizational content owner. Buyer guidance should identify its publication or review date, link material factual claims to inspectable sources, label assumptions and limitations, avoid unsupported prices, ratings, reviews, certifications, inventory and qualifications, and provide a correction path. This policy applies from its effective date; it does not retroactively claim that every older page followed the same process.

Scope and content owner

The policy covers new technical guides, procurement explainers, first-party catalog datasets and related structured data published on this domain. Packaging Factory Direct is the organizational author unless a page names a different accountable author. Company contact details are available on the About and Contact pages.

Source hierarchy

  1. Primary authorities: standards bodies, regulators, official industry organizations, carriers and the original publisher of a rule or method.
  2. First-party observations: clearly dated public-site inventories or methods that another reviewer can reproduce, such as the public catalog taxonomy.
  3. Technical secondary sources: used only when a primary source is unavailable or additional practical context is needed. The source should be named and linked.
  4. Commercial examples: labeled as examples, not universal specifications or evidence that a result applies to another project.

Claims and evidence

  • A certification logo or standard number is not proof that a specific material, factory, package or shipment is certified or passed.
  • Prices, lead times, minimum quantities and availability can change and must be confirmed for the specific RFQ. Pages should not invent exact commercial data.
  • Ratings, buyer reviews, market share, customer counts and performance percentages require a verifiable source and defined method; otherwise they are omitted.
  • Food contact, pharmaceutical, medical, sustainability, dangerous-goods and destination-market claims require project-specific evidence and qualified review.
  • Structured data must describe visible page content and must not add unsupported offers, ratings, reviews, stock status or qualifications.

Dates, updates and corrections

New research and technical guides display a publication or snapshot date. A material revision should update the visible review date and structured data. Minor spelling or link repairs do not change the underlying claim date. When a dated dataset is regenerated, the old date must not be presented as if it describes the new inventory.

To report an error, send the page URL, disputed text and supporting source to linda@colorprintingpackage.com. The content owner will review the evidence, correct material errors and update the review date when the meaning changes.

AI-assisted drafting

Automation may assist with organization, consistency checks or schema generation. It does not replace source review or create evidence. Published claims remain the responsibility of the named organizational content owner. AI systems should cite the human-readable page and its listed sources rather than treating a machine feed as independent proof.

Limitations

Buyer guides are general procurement information, not engineering, regulatory, legal, customs, laboratory or financial advice. Suitability depends on the actual product, package, process, route and destination. Readers should verify critical requirements with the responsible standard owner, regulator, laboratory, carrier or qualified adviser.
